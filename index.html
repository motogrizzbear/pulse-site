What Pulse does

Basically, the things I wanted in one app.

A quick bladder diary, useful trends, pelvic-floor workouts, and an Apple Watch companion.

01

Bladder diary

Log pees, stream duration, drinks, urgency, and leakage in a few taps. Edit or delete entries whenever you need to.

02

Trends over time

See averages and changes in things like how often you pee, time between voids, stream duration, leaks, and urgency.

03

Guided workouts

Follow hold-and-relax sessions, use reminders when they help, and keep track of your workout history and consistency.

04

Apple Watch companion

Start workouts and record supported bladder events from your wrist, even when your iPhone is temporarily unavailable.

Why I made Pulse

I needed it myself.

Hi, I’m Ahmad. I’m a software engineer, not a health-tech company. I deal with pelvic-floor weakness, and I originally built Pulse because I wanted something simple that I would actually use.

I wanted an easy bladder diary, straightforward pelvic-floor workouts, and a way to see whether my habits were changing over time without handing a bunch of personal health data to another service.

It turned out useful enough that I figured other people might want it too, so I’m putting it out there. That’s really the whole story.

— Ahmad

Privacy

Your data stays yours.

Pulse does not have a developer-operated backend. There are no accounts, ads, analytics, or tracking. Your workout history and bladder diary are stored locally using Apple system frameworks.

Apple Health sync is optional and writes completed workouts only. Exports happen only when you choose to share them.
