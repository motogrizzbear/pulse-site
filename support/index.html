Help & Support

Using Pulse

A practical guide to bladder logging, trends, workouts, Apple Watch sync, reminders, Health access, exports, and common troubleshooting.

On this page Getting started Bladder diary Trends Workouts Apple Watch Reminders & Health Export & delete Troubleshooting Contact

Getting started

Pulse opens to the Bladder Log so the most common diary actions are immediately available. The other main areas are Workouts, History, and Settings.

  • Bladder Log is for quick diary entries and day-by-day review.
  • Workouts contains the Guided Program, workout plans, and today’s checklist.
  • History shows bladder trends, daily summaries, and workout history.
  • Settings controls reminders, Apple Health sync, and the personal overnight reporting window.

Bladder diary

Log a pee

Choose Log Pee, enter the stream duration, optionally record urgency, and save. Stream duration is the number of seconds you urinated; it is not a measured urine volume.

Log a drink

Choose Log Drink, select a beverage type and amount, then save. Fluid totals are shown in ounces.

Log leakage or urgency

You can record leakage severity and an optional activity context, or add an urgency-only event when there was no void.

Edit or delete an entry

Tap an existing bladder-diary row to edit it. Swipe to delete when you want to remove a record. Editing keeps the same underlying entry identity instead of creating a duplicate.

Overnight reporting window

Pulse uses a configurable personal overnight reporting window, defaulting to 11 PM–7 AM, to separate daytime and overnight events in summaries. This is a reporting preference, not a clinical diagnosis or nocturia assessment.

Understanding trends

History can compare your recent logging period with the immediately preceding period. You can switch among 7-, 14-, and 28-day comparisons and review measures such as voids per day, average interval, average stream duration, leaks per day, and urgency signals.

The trend chart supports multiple time ranges and grouping levels. Use it to look for changes in your own routine rather than as a diagnostic result.

Logging coverage matters. A low-coverage period may reflect missing diary entries rather than a true behavior change.

Pelvic-floor workouts

The Guided Program provides structured progression. Standard, Quick, and custom workouts are also available when you want a different session.

  • Follow the on-screen HOLD and RELAX phases.
  • Completed workouts contribute to normal history, streaks, and weekly goals.
  • Only completed Guided Program sessions advance Guided Program progression.
  • If you stop early, Pulse records the intended incomplete state rather than treating the session as fully completed.

Apple Watch

The Apple Watch companion can receive workout plans from your iPhone and send completed workouts and supported bladder-log entries back to the phone.

If your iPhone is temporarily unreachable, Pulse uses Apple’s WatchConnectivity delivery mechanisms to queue supported records for later delivery. Reopen the apps and bring the devices back within normal connectivity range if a record has not appeared yet.

Do not repeatedly re-log the same event while waiting for sync. Give the devices a chance to reconnect first.

Reminders and Apple Health

Workout reminders

Workout reminders are scheduled locally on your device. If notifications are denied, Pulse remains usable; you can change notification permissions later in iOS Settings.

Apple Health

Health sync is optional. When enabled and authorized, Pulse can write completed workouts to Apple Health. Pulse does not read your existing Health data.

Exporting and deleting data

CSV export

History exports are created only when you request them. iOS then presents the standard share sheet so you can choose where the CSV goes. After sharing, the destination you selected is responsible for that copy.

Delete data

You can delete individual records or use the available Clear All controls for workout history or bladder logs. Deleting the Pulse app removes its locally stored app data. Workouts already written to Apple Health remain under your control in the Health app.

Troubleshooting

A Watch record has not appeared on iPhone

  1. Confirm the iPhone and Watch are paired and have normal connectivity.
  2. Open Pulse on both devices.
  3. Allow a short period for Apple’s background transfer to complete.
  4. Avoid creating a duplicate entry for the same event while the original may still be queued.

Notifications are not appearing

Check the notification permission shown in Pulse Settings, then verify Pulse is allowed to send notifications in iOS Settings. Reminder scheduling is local and does not use a Pulse push-notification server.

Health workouts are not syncing

Verify Health sync is enabled in Pulse and that write permission for workouts is allowed in Apple Health settings. A denied permission does not prevent you from using Pulse normally.

The app looks stale after a change

Force-quit Pulse and relaunch it. If the issue persists, restarting the affected device can help clear transient Apple-platform state.

The website lives in the public Pulse site repository. If you found a site or app bug that does not include sensitive personal information, you can cut an issue.

Medical note

Pulse is a personal tracking and exercise-guidance tool. It does not provide medical advice, diagnose medical conditions, or replace care from a physician, pelvic-health professional, or other qualified clinician. If you have pain, blood in your urine, inability to urinate, new loss of bladder control, or another urgent concern, seek appropriate medical care.
